PixiJS NPOT Texture Mipmapping Visual Artifacts

Older versions of Pixi.js utilizing WebGL 1.0 backends encounter strict hardware limitations when handling non-power-of-two (NPOT) textures alongside mipmapping. In WebGL 1.0, generating mipmaps on textures whose dimensions are not powers of two (such as 256×256 or 512×512) violates the specification unless specific hardware extensions are active. When Pixi.js attempts to generate mipmaps for NPOT assets in these environments, the texture fails WebGL's completeness checks, leading to severe visual corruption, missing graphics, or distorted rendering during runtime.

Solid Black Rectangles

The most prevalent artifact is the complete replacement of the sprite with a solid black box. Under the WebGL 1.0 specification, calling gl.generateMipmap() on an NPOT texture triggers a GL_INVALID_OPERATION error. This failure leaves the texture "incomplete." When a fragment shader samples an incomplete WebGL texture, the specification dictates that it must return opaque black (rgba(0, 0, 0, 1)). As a result, characters, UI elements, or background assets suddenly render as solid black silhouettes corresponding to the quad's bounding box.

Disappearing Sprites and Invisibility

Depending on the blend mode and shader implementation, an incomplete texture can sample as transparent black (rgba(0, 0, 0, 0)). In standard alpha-blended rendering pipelines, this causes the sprite to become completely invisible. The geometry continues to be processed by the GPU and impacts draw call counts, but the visual output drops out entirely, making elements randomly vanish when scaled down or viewed at distances that trigger lower mip levels.

Aspect Ratio Distortion and Texture Squishing

To avoid WebGL 1.0 NPOT failures, older Pixi.js iterations sometimes utilized automatic scaling routines, resizing or padding NPOT textures to the nearest power of two (POT) via a hidden HTML5 canvas. If this process fails to update the UV texture coordinates accurately, visual distortion occurs:

  • Stretching and Squishing: Sprites appear distorted along the horizontal or vertical axes as the non-square asset is forced into a square POT dimension.
  • Bilinear Blurring: Resampling the canvas to POT introduces interpolation artifacts, resulting in a loss of crisp pixel detail and creating a soft, smeared appearance across high-contrast edges.

Edge Bleeding and Clamping Artifacts

NPOT textures in WebGL 1.0 strictly require the texture wrap parameters TEXTURE_WRAP_S and TEXTURE_WRAP_T to be set to CLAMP_TO_EDGE. If an application relies on repeating textures or spritesheets without adequate padding:

  • Outer pixel rows and columns stretch endlessly along the geometry boundaries.
  • Extraneous color fringes bleed into neighboring frames on an atlas due to linear filtering sampling beyond the intended sprite bounds.

Rendering Flickering

Visual instability frequently manifests as rapid flickering between the expected image and a black square. This occurs when dynamic scaling crosses a threshold: while a sprite is displayed at its native 1:1 scale, the pipeline may avoid referencing mipmaps, rendering correctly. As soon as the object scales down, the minification filter invokes the invalid mipmap chain, instantly rendering the texture incomplete and causing it to flicker between its actual graphic and a blank state.
