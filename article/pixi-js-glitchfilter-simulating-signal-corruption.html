Pixi.js GlitchFilter: Simulating Signal Corruption

The GlitchFilter in Pixi.js simulates digital video signal corruption by running a specialized WebGL fragment shader that programmatically degrades an object's rendered texture. Through a combination of horizontal slice displacement, RGB channel separation (chromatic aberration), and dynamic pseudo-random noise, the filter recreates the visual artifacts typical of malfunctioning digital displays, damaged video cables, and compression sync errors.

Horizontal Slice Displacement

At the core of the filter's tearing effect is the manipulation of UV texture coordinates. The fragment shader subdivides the target display object vertically into a configurable number of horizontal bands or "slices." Instead of sampling pixels from their original coordinates, the shader applies a randomized horizontal offset to distinct slices. This shifts pixel rows left or right independently, producing the jagged, torn appearance associated with horizontal synchronization failures in digital video processing.

Chromatic Aberration

Digital transmission errors often result in misaligned color channels. The GlitchFilter replicates this effect by sampling the Red, Green, and Blue color channels from distinct coordinate positions rather than a single unified UV coordinate. The shader offsets the red channel in one direction and the blue channel in another while keeping the green channel relatively centered. This creates distinct color fringing—often visible as cyan and magenta separation along the edges of high-contrast graphics.

Pseudo-Random Noise and Time Updates

Real-world signal corruption is chaotic and continuously shifting. The filter relies on internal uniform variables, such as seed and offset, which are calculated using deterministic pseudo-random functions inside the GLSL shader. When these uniforms are updated per frame via the application's render loop, the positions, widths, and displacement strengths of the horizontal slices regenerate rapidly. This produces the erratic flickering and artifact jumps typical of an active, unstable video feed.

Configurable Distortion Properties

The simulation can be adjusted to mimic different types of hardware or digital failures through exposed properties:

  • slices: Dictates the count of horizontal divisions, ranging from a few large blocks to hundreds of fine scanline tears.
  • offset: Sets the maximum distance that slices are displaced horizontally.
  • direction: Adjusts the angle and drift vector of the distortion lines.
  • fillMode: Defines how the shader renders newly exposed areas where pixels were shifted away (e.g., clamping edges, mirroring, or looping texture data).
  • red, green, and blue: Allows independent coordinate adjustment vectors for each color channel to emphasize or minimize chromatic separation.

By calculating coordinate displacement and color offsets entirely on the GPU, the GlitchFilter provides a performant, real-time recreation of faulty digital video hardware.
