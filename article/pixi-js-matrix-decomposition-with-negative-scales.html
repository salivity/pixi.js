Pixi.js Matrix Decomposition with Negative Scales

In Pixi.js, decomposing a transformation matrix back into human-readable properties—such as position, scale, rotation, and skew—encounters mathematical ambiguity when negative scale values are involved. Because Pixi.js calculates scale magnitudes using Euclidean vector lengths, negative scales are inherently lost during decomposition and become positive values. Depending on whether one or both axes are inverted, Pixi.js absorbs the resulting reflection into either an altered rotation or a large skew offset rather than restoring the negative scale values.

The Mathematics Behind Pixi.js Decomposition

Pixi.js represents 2D affine transformations using a \(3 \times 3\) matrix with six active components: a, b, c, d, tx, and ty. When an object's matrix is updated, these values are computed from its position, scale, rotation, and skew.

When reversing this process via Matrix.decompose(), Pixi.js reconstructs the scale vectors using the Pythagorean theorem:

transform.scale.x = Math.sqrt((a * a) + (b * b));
transform.scale.y = Math.sqrt((c * c) + (d * d));

Because the square root function strictly returns non-negative real numbers, the resulting transform.scale.x and transform.scale.y will always be greater than or equal to zero.

Case 1: Both Scales Are Negative

When both scale.x and scale.y are negative (for example, scale.set(-1, -1)), the matrix determinant remains positive (\(ad - bc > 0\)). Geometrically, scaling an object by \(-1\) on both axes is identical to rotating that object by 180 degrees (\(\pi\) radians).

During decomposition:

  1. scale.x and scale.y both evaluate to positive magnitudes.
  2. The internal angles skewX and skewY align such that their combined delta satisfies Pixi's condition for pure rotation.
  3. Pixi.js assigns the \(180^\circ\) difference directly to transform.rotation.
  4. Skew remains 0.

The visual output remains correct, but the transform state is represented as positive scales accompanied by a \(180^\circ\) rotation.

Case 2: A Single Axis Is Negative (Reflection)

When only one axis has a negative scale (such as scale.x = -1 and scale.y = 1), the transformation produces a negative determinant (\(ad - bc < 0\)). This introduces a true reflection (mirroring) that cannot be reproduced using pure rotation and positive scales alone.

During decomposition:

  1. scale.x and scale.y are once again forced positive via Math.sqrt().
  2. Pixi.js computes skewX and skewY. Because of the reflection, the angles do not cancel out into a standard rotation.
  3. Pixi.js falls back to its skew branch: rotation is set to 0, while the reflection is absorbed into skew.x or skew.y as an offset of \(\pi\) radians (\(180^\circ\)).

Practical Implications

When you rely on matrix.decompose(transform) to sync parent matrices, physics bodies, or interpolation engines, objects flipped across a single axis will not reflect -1 in their scale properties. Instead, you will observe positive scale values combined with an unexpected \(180^\circ\) skew.

To preserve explicit negative scale values, track the original scale sign separately, or check the determinant of the matrix (\(a \cdot d - b \cdot c\)). If the determinant is negative, you must manually invert one of the decomposed scale axes and subtract the corresponding \(\pi\) adjustment from the rotation or skew.
