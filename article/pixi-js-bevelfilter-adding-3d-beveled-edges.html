Pixi.js BevelFilter: Adding 3D Beveled Edges

The BevelFilter in PixiJS simulates three-dimensional depth on flat 2D display objects by generating directional highlights and shadows along the edges of their alpha masks. Through a custom fragment shader, the filter evaluates the boundaries of a shape, applies a virtual light source angle, and renders contrasting bright and dark borders to create the illusion of raised, beveled surfaces without modifying the underlying 2D geometry.

How the Fragment Shader Detects Edges

The BevelFilter—available via the @pixi/filter-bevel package—operates entirely in screen space using GPU fragment shaders. Because 2D sprites and graphics lack surface normals or z-coordinates, the filter relies on the object's alpha (transparency) channel to determine where edges exist:

  1. Alpha Thresholding: The shader samples the alpha value of the current pixel. If the pixel is fully transparent, it remains untouched.
  2. Offset Sampling: To determine whether a pixel is near an outer edge, the shader samples neighboring pixels at specific distances defined by the thickness property.
  3. Difference Calculation: By comparing the alpha value of the current pixel against neighboring pixels offset in opposing directions, the shader identifies transitions from opaque pixels to transparent boundaries.

Simulating Directional Light and Shadow

Once an edge is detected, the filter applies light and shadow depending on the orientation of that edge relative to a configured light source:

  • Highlight Generation: The shader checks an offset position oriented towards the light angle (rotation). If the offset pixel has lower opacity than the current pixel, it indicates that this edge faces the virtual light source. The pixel is then blended with the lightColor modulated by lightAlpha.
  • Shadow Generation: Conversely, the shader checks an offset position oriented away from the light source. If an edge boundary is detected facing away from the light, the shader applies the shadowColor modulated by shadowAlpha.

By simultaneously producing bright highlights on the edge facing the light and dark tones on the opposing edge, the human eye interprets the sudden luminance gradient as a sloped, light-reflecting 3D facet.

Key Configurable Properties

The appearance and intensity of the bevel effect are controlled through several specific parameters:

  • rotation (in degrees): Defines the angle of the incident light source. For instance, an angle of 45 degrees positions the virtual light in the top-right corner, casting highlights on top-right edges and shadows on bottom-left edges.
  • thickness (in pixels): Determines how far the shader samples from the primary pixel, directly controlling the perceived width and steepness of the bevel.
  • lightColor and shadowColor: Hexadecimal values defining the colors of the illuminated and shaded sides. Defaults are typically white (0xffffff) and black (0x000000).
  • lightAlpha and shadowAlpha: Floating-point values between 0.0 and 1.0 that control the opacity and blending intensity of the highlights and shadows.

Performance Considerations

Because the BevelFilter evaluates multi-tap texture offsets per pixel within a fragment shader, it executes efficiently on modern GPUs. It provides an immediate visual upgrade for UI buttons, text, and flat vector artwork without the overhead of generating 3D meshes, computing lighting normal maps, or handling complex scene illumination.
