Elastic Camera Bounce Math in Pixi.js

Creating an elastic camera boundary bounce in Pixi.js requires simulating a damped harmonic oscillator, a classic physics model governed by Hooke's Law combined with velocity damping. When the camera moves beyond the defined world boundaries, mathematical formulas calculate a restoring spring force proportional to the penetration distance, alongside a counteracting damping force based on the camera's speed. These forces are converted into acceleration and integrated over time within the Pixi.js ticker loop, causing the camera viewport to decelerate outside the boundary and snap back smoothly without infinite oscillation.

1. Boundary Penetration (Displacement)

In Pixi.js, a camera is generally represented by a root Container holding world objects, shifted inversely to the camera's target coordinates (\(x_{container} = -x_{camera}\)).

The first step is determining displacement (\(x\)), which measures how far the camera has crossed a boundary:

  • Left Boundary (\(x_{min}\)): If \(x_{camera} < x_{min}\), displacement is \(x = x_{camera} - x_{min}\) (a negative value).
  • Right Boundary (\(x_{max}\)): If \(x_{camera} > x_{max}\), displacement is \(x = x_{camera} - x_{max}\) (a positive value).

If the camera is inside the boundaries, displacement is zero, and no spring forces are applied.

2. The Restoring Force (Hooke's Law)

Once the camera exits the boundaries, Hooke's Law is used to calculate the restoring force (\(F_s\)) pulling it back toward the valid region:

\[F_s = -k \cdot x\]

  • \(k\) is the spring stiffness coefficient. Higher values create a stiffer, faster snap-back, while lower values allow deeper penetration into the margin.
  • \(x\) is the boundary displacement calculated in the previous step.

The negative sign ensures that the resulting force pushes directly opposite to the direction of boundary penetration.

3. Viscous Damping Force

Without friction, Hooke’s Law produces perpetual oscillation. To ensure the camera settles smoothly back at the boundary, a damping force (\(F_d\)) is introduced:

\[F_d = -c \cdot v\]

  • \(c\) is the damping coefficient.
  • \(v\) is the current velocity of the camera.

This force opposes the current motion of the camera, shedding kinetic energy and eliminating jitter. For smooth settling without excessive bounce, the system should approach critical damping:

\[c \approx 2 \sqrt{k \cdot m}\]

Where \(m\) is the virtual mass of the camera (typically set to \(1\) for simplicity).

4. Integration via the Ticker Loop

Pixi.js runs an internal render loop (app.ticker.add) passing a delta time parameter (\(\Delta t\)). Newton's second law (\(F = m \cdot a\)) translates the net force into acceleration:

\[a = \frac{F_s + F_d}{m}\]

Assuming \(m = 1\):

\[a = (-k \cdot x) + (-c \cdot v)\]

Using Semi-Implicit Euler integration, the velocity and position update per frame as follows:

  1. Update Velocity: \(v_{new} = v_{current} + (a \cdot \Delta t)\)
  2. Update Camera Position: \(x_{camera\_new} = x_{camera} + (v_{new} \cdot \Delta t)\)
  3. Update Pixi Container: container.x = -x_camera_new

Applying this calculation independently to both the X and Y axes allows the camera to bounce naturally off corners as well as straight edges.
