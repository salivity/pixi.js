PixiJS cacheAsBitmap and Transform Calculations

Setting cacheAsBitmap to true fundamentally alters how Pixi.js processes matrix transformations by decoupling a container's children from the scene graph’s continuous transform pipeline. Instead of recursively multiplying local transformation matrices down the hierarchy each frame, Pixi.js bakes the descendants into an isolated coordinate system on a render texture. Consequently, the container stops executing transform updates across its child tree and instead applies its scene-level world transform directly to a single cached quad.

The Standard Transform Lifecycle

Under standard operation, Pixi.js relies on the updateTransform() method. Every frame, the scene graph traverses from the root container down to each leaf node. For every display object, Pixi.js multiplies its local transform matrix (combining position, scale, rotation, skew, and pivot) by its parent’s worldTransform matrix. In deeply nested or populated containers, computing these matrix multiplications consumes significant CPU cycles.

How cacheAsBitmap Intercepts Transforms

When cacheAsBitmap is enabled, Pixi.js changes how and when transforms are evaluated:

  1. Local Frame Isolation: Pixi.js calculates the local bounding box of all descendants. It then renders the children into an off-screen RenderTexture. During this single bake pass, children's transforms are computed relative to the cache's local bounds rather than the global stage coordinates.
  2. Transform Freezing: Once the texture is generated, the children are removed from the regular rendering and transform loop. Their individual localTransform and worldTransform matrices are no longer recalculated every frame, even if their properties (such as position or rotation) change programmatically.
  3. Delegation to an Internal Proxy: Pixi.js instantiates an internal cached sprite to represent the container's contents. The parent container's worldTransform is assigned directly to this proxy quad.

Matrix Operations During Rendering

Once cached, the parent container still updates its own worldTransform relative to its ancestors. If the parent moves, rotates, or scales, Pixi.js recalculates the parent's matrix normally.

However, instead of propagating this matrix downward to update dozens or hundreds of child matrices, the GPU handles the transformation as a single textured quad draw call. The internal contents behave as fixed pixels rather than independent vector or sprite nodes, shifting the workload from CPU-bound matrix multiplication to standard GPU texture sampling.

Behavioral Consequences for Transformations

Because the internal transform calculations are bypassed, several operational changes occur:

  • Global Coordinate Queries: Calling methods like toGlobal() or toLocal() on children of a cached container may return outdated or incorrect values because child world matrices are no longer updated relative to the stage.
  • Child Transform Invalidation: Any transform changes made directly to children (e.g., updating child.x or child.rotation) will not visually reflect on the screen until cacheAsBitmap is set to false and re-enabled to force a re-bake.
  • Scaling Artifacts: Scaling the parent container transforms the baked texture rather than recalculating child geometry. Scaling up will produce visual pixelation or blurring rather than sharp re-rasterized elements, because the underlying transform matrices of the children remain frozen at the resolution at which they were originally cached.
