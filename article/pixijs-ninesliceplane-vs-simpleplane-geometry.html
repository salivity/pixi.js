PixiJS NineSlicePlane vs SimplePlane Geometry

In PixiJS, both NineSlicePlane and SimplePlane inherit from Mesh, yet their underlying vertex geometries serve fundamentally different rendering purposes. While a SimplePlane constructs an arbitrary, uniformly distributed grid of vertices designed for mesh deformation and wave effects, a NineSlicePlane implements a rigid 4×4 vertex layout explicitly tuned for non-uniform 9-slice UI scaling. Understanding how their vertex positions, counts, and spacing mechanics differ is essential for choosing the right mesh structure for your scene.

Grid Topology and Vertex Counts

The most immediate geometric divergence between the two planes is grid dimension and total vertex count:

  • SimplePlane: The topology is user-defined. When instantiated, you define the mesh resolution via verticesX and verticesY. The total number of vertices equals verticesX * verticesY. For example, a 10×10 segment plane produces a grid of 100 vertices (10 vertices wide by 10 vertices high).
  • NineSlicePlane: The topology is strictly static. It always consists of a 4×4 grid of vertices, producing exactly 16 vertices that divide the plane into 9 distinct rectangular patches (quads) formed by 18 triangles (or 32 indices).

Vertex Coordinate Distribution

The physical placement and spacing of the vertices across the coordinate space define how each plane handles space:

  • SimplePlane (Uniform Spacing): Vertices are calculated linearly across the width and height of the plane. The distance between each adjacent column of vertices is uniformly width / (verticesX - 1), and the distance between adjacent rows is height / (verticesY - 1). In local space, the vertices are spaced evenly across the entire surface area.
  • NineSlicePlane (Margin-Driven Spacing): Vertices are non-uniformly distributed based on four explicit boundary properties: leftWidth, rightWidth, topHeight, and bottomHeight.
    • Column 0 sits at 0.
    • Column 1 sits at leftWidth.
    • Column 2 sits at width - rightWidth.
    • Column 3 sits at width.
    • Row 0 sits at 0.
    • Row 1 sits at topHeight.
    • Row 2 sits at height - bottomHeight.
    • Row 3 sits at height.

Because of this arrangement, the distance between index 0 and 1, or index 2 and 3, remains constant regardless of the mesh's total dimensions, while the distance between index 1 and 2 stretches or shrinks dynamically.

Transformation and Resize Behavior

When resizing or manipulating these objects at runtime, their vertices react differently:

  • SimplePlane: Scaling or resizing recalculates all vertex positions proportionally, maintaining the relative ratio between all segments across the mesh. If you displace individual vertices in the vertex buffer, the deformation smoothly interpolates across adjacent triangles, making it ideal for simulating physics, cloth, ribbons, or water surfaces.
  • NineSlicePlane: When the dimensions (width or height) change, only the inner four vertices (columns 1 and 2, rows 1 and 2) translate along the modified axis. The four outer corner regions preserve their absolute geometric sizes and aspect ratios, preventing distortion of UI border artwork, while the middle edge vertices stretch on one axis and the central quad stretches across both axes.
